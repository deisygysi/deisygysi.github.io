I am a Professor in the [Department of Statistics](http://www.est.ufpr.br/) and head of the Precision Analytics Lab at the Federal University of Paraná ([UFPR](https://ufpr.br/)), Brazil. I also supervise graduate students in UFPR's [Bioinformatics](https://www.bioinfo.ufpr.br/) and [Informatics](https://www.prppg.ufpr.br/site/ppginf/pb/) programs. My research sits at the intersection of **Network Science**, **Machine Learning**, and **Precision Medicine**, where I develop computational approaches to understand disease mechanisms and support more targeted diagnostics and therapies.

My work combines molecular, clinical, and population-level data to study complex disease. I am particularly interested in network-based biomarker discovery, drug repurposing, and the contribution of non-coding RNA interactions to disease diagnosis, progression, and treatment. I also develop open methods and R packages that help researchers analyze and interpret complex biomedical data.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, I contributed to a [network-medicine framework for drug repurposing](https://www.pnas.org/content/118/19/e2025581118), identifying candidate treatments for SARS-CoV-2 infection. I also contributed to the [20th anniversary of the Human Genome Project](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-021-00314-6) in *Nature*. More recently, my research has shown how [non-coding RNAs expand the predictive power of Network Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2301342120), with applications ranging from [cancer biology](/publications/rhabdolncrna/) to [the future of network biology](/publications/netbiodirections/).

Before joining UFPR, I was an Associate Research Scientist at the [BarabasiLab](https://www.barabasilab.com/) and the Network Science Institute at Northeastern University. I was also a Research Trainee at the Channing Division of Network Medicine at Harvard Medical School and Brigham and Women's Hospital, and a Bioinformatician at Veterans Affairs. These experiences shaped my interest in using network-based approaches to investigate autoimmune conditions, mental health, and age-related diseases.

I hold Bachelor's degrees in Biotechnology from the Pontifical Catholic University and in Statistics from UFPR. During my undergraduate studies, I received two awards for research projects and worked as a Risk Analyst at HSBC. I later worked as a Bioinformatician at the Heart Institute of the University of São Paulo (USP), studying co-expression networks and the effects of single nucleotide polymorphisms on them, and as a Statistician at Albert Einstein Hospital, advising its research department.

I completed my Ph.D. at the University of Leipzig, Germany, under the supervision of [Dr. Peter Stadler](http://www.bioinf.uni-leipzig.de/), [Dr. Katja Nowick](http://www.nowick-lab.info/?author=1), and [Dr. Martin Middendorf](http://pacosy.informatik.uni-leipzig.de/1-1-Home.html). Today, I lead research and training at UFPR with the goal of making network-based approaches useful, transparent, and impactful in biomedicine.
